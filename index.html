Questions, answered

What's a go-around?

An airliner on final approach climbs away instead of landing and flies another approach — a routine safety procedure when a runway isn't clear or an approach isn't stable. Spotters call it a go-around or a missed approach. They're unplanned, they're dramatic, and they're uncommon enough that catching one live is special.

Which airports will be covered?

We're launching with Los Angeles (LAX), New York (JFK), London Heathrow (LHR), Frankfurt (FRA) and Amsterdam (AMS). Each airport gets its own free public channel, and more airports follow as the service grows.

How do alerts arrive?

Free: public Telegram channels and Discord feeds, with a short delay. Instant: private alerts delivered the moment it happens. Every alert — free or Instant — shows the airline, aircraft type, airport and time, with direct links to the aircraft on ADS-B Exchange and FlightRadar24.

What's the difference between Free and Instant?

Free channels post the go-arounds we detect at each covered airport with a short delay. Instant is $4.99/month or $39.99/year: alerts the moment it happens, up to 10 airports at once, and airline and aircraft-type filters.

Do I need an app?

No. Alerts arrive in Telegram or Discord — join the free channels or connect your account for Instant alerts and you're set.

Are you affiliated with ADS-B Exchange or FlightRadar24?

No — we're an independent service. Alerts link to the aircraft's pages on ADS-B Exchange and FlightRadar24 so you can pull them up and watch; we're not affiliated with or endorsed by either.

Privacy details

Who is responsible

This site is run by Canvas Tech Solutions, which is responsible for the data described here.

The anonymous visit count

Before you answer, each page view is counted as one anonymous row: the page path, any traffic-source (utm) tags, the referring site when present, device type (mobile, desktop or tablet) and the time — nothing else is kept, on your device or ours.

What we collect if you allow analytics

  • Campaign tags from the page address, when present: utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_id, utm_content and utm_term. Other tags and ad-click IDs (like gclid or fbclid) are ignored.
  • The page address in a cleaned form: the site, the page path and the accepted campaign tags — not the full address with every parameter.
  • Basic browser/device details: device type, browser family and major version, operating-system family, a screen-size range, and your browser's main language. We don't record the raw browser identification string and we don't fingerprint your device.
  • A random browser ID, kept for up to 30 days: a random code stored for this site only. It's not your name, email or account — but it can be linked to a signup you make here, so it isn't anonymous.
  • A session ID for the current tab, which expires after 30 minutes of inactivity.
  • Visit records: one record per page view measured after you allow analytics, kept for up to 90 days.

How analytics connects to signups

If you allow analytics and later submit the launch-notification form on this site, your visit can be connected to that signup, so we can see which campaign brought you here. If you decline or haven't chosen, signing up works exactly the same — we just store the page address without campaign tags, and no visit connection is made. Allowing analytics is never required to sign up.

Service providers

  • This site's pages are hosted by GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.).
  • Signup and analytics records are stored in a PostgreSQL database hosted in Ontario, on Microsoft Azure. Other parts of the service — such as backups or support — may involve processing outside Ontario.
  • The launch-notification form is protected by Google reCAPTCHA Enterprise, a separate security service from Google that checks submissions for abuse. It runs whether or not you allow analytics; declining analytics doesn't turn it off.

Why we use this data

  • To measure which campaigns bring visits and launch-notification signups on this site.
  • To spot coarse browser and device compatibility issues.

What we don't do

  • No advertising trackers or ad pixels.
  • No cross-site identity — your browser ID works on this site only.
  • No joining analytics data to your email address.
  • No fingerprinting.
  • No full IP address in analytics records. Like any website, this site's ordinary hosting and security do receive standard network details such as your IP address — that's separate from the optional analytics choice.
  • If you decline: we store only your choice and its date on your browser — no browser ID, no visit records.

How long we keep it

  • Random browser ID: up to 30 days.
  • Detailed visit records and signup links: up to 90 days.
  • Your privacy choice itself: up to 180 days, or until we make a material change to this notice.
  • Summary counts without browser IDs (for example, visits per campaign per day): up to 13 months.

Changing your choice

Select 'Privacy choices' anytime. Declining or withdrawing stops collection immediately, clears the browser ID and discards any not-yet-sent visits. Records already made are kept until their normal expiry (above) unless you ask us to delete them.

Access and deletion

You can ask what data is connected to your browser ID, or have it deleted, by contacting the site owner (Canvas Tech Solutions). Because a browser ID alone doesn't prove who you are, we use a basic verification step before sharing or deleting anything.
